## What this is

**The defect (item 361).** Retirement and the compromise declaration are per kid. A rotation authorized by a live key that names a retired, or compromised, key's public bytes under a **new kid** is honoured by the Forum's fold: nothing compares key material across kids (`LifecycleFold.Rotation` checks `_bound.ContainsKey(statement.Kid)` and stops; `RotateKey` step 8 checks `KeyActs.AnyEntryNames(all, rotation.Kid)`, kid-only; `KeyEnrollment.SameMaterial` and `ClosedJwk.SamePublicKey` exist and are used only for one kid's orphan row and for declaration keys). The words PR's K5 measured it through the real use case. Cybersecurity ruling: low to medium; no escape for a thief holding only the compromised key (the row needs a live key of the same identity, or an external `--signer` that holds the bytes); not a beta blocker; **a precondition of the guard's lift**. Owner, 2026-11-24, option A: refuse such bytes in the fold, in `RotateKey` and in the client's signer path, with a vector agreed by C#, `curia-testis` and the readers.

**The rule, decided (ruling 1).** A rotation is not honoured when the public key its statement names is the public key of **any key the honoured prefix of that identity's stream has ever bound, under any kid, whether it is now active, retired or compromised** (including a key a declaration compromised by cascade). "Bound" is by the honoured prefix only: a row the fold skipped binds nothing, so bytes stated only by a skipped row are free to be bound (the over-broad reading would hand any appender a way to block an identity's future key). The comparison is on the key (algorithm and every coordinate), never on the kid.

**Class:** a rule implemented faithfully per name that guards nothing per key: the project's named failure mode, in its security form. The probes this plan needs are of two kinds the project has paid for: vectors whose verdict is written from the construction and never by running a verifier, and probes that are red on today's tree **by their own assertion** (a kid-only control is a green that proves nothing).

## Rulings (decided, not asked)

1. **What is refused: key material, honoured prefix, any status** (above). ## The errata entry (G78), proposed text

Appended **after G77** in the errata, status "specified; not built" until Task 8. Pasted by Task 1 after the controller's immediate re-read of the file. The Status paragraph is written from the run by Task 8, its shape G77's.

> ## G78 — A key's bytes are bound once: a rotation that names the public key of any key the honoured prefix has bound is refused by the route and skipped by every fold, whatever the key's standing and under whatever kid, and `curia rotate --signer` refuses a signer whose key the key set lists
>
> **Location.** R4.18 (revised) (what refuses a new kid), R4.39 and R6.57 (what retirement and compromise end), R6.64 (the Forum's fold), R6.54 (the readers' fold), R11.105 (the retirement help's permanence); register item 361; G77 non-property (d).
>
> **How it surfaced.** The words PR's Task 0 (K5), checking whether a sentence "no lifecycle entry reinstates a key" was true, built a rotation authorized by a live key that names a retired key's public bytes under a new kid, and the real `RotateKey` and fold honoured it (index 6, the new kid active). The cybersecurity ruling of 2026-11-24 put it at low to medium: a thief who holds only the compromised key cannot make the row (the authorizer must be a live key of the same identity, or a signer that holds the bytes), and the operator can declare the new kid compromised. The owner decided on 2026-11-24 to refuse it.
>
> **Argument.** Retirement and the compromise declaration end a key; the Forum's rules named the key by its `kid`, and a `kid` is a label the binder chooses. A fold that compares labels makes "retired" and "compromised" statements about a name: the bytes a declaration was written to stop signing come back under another name with the identity's own authorization, and every post signed under them verifies. The remedy is the property the words already claimed: a key an identity has bound is bound once. The rule is over the **honoured prefix**, because a row the fold skipped binds nothing (R6.64) and a rule over every row ever appended would let anyone who can append block an identity's next key by stating its bytes in a forged row. It is over the key, not the kid: the kid check stays, as its own rule, because a label reused for new bytes is a different defect (R4.18 (revised)).
>
> ### Requirement text
>
> **R4.45** A rotation SHALL be refused by name, and a rotation row SHALL NOT be honoured, when the public key its statement names (its algorithm and every coordinate, never its `kid`) is the public key of any key the honoured prefix of the identity's stream has bound (R6.64), under any `kid`, whether that key is live, retired, declared compromised or compromised by cascade (R6.57). The route's refusal SHALL be the problem type `curia/keys/key-already-bound`, status `409`, whose `detail` names no `kid`, no key and no statement; it SHALL be decided after the refusal for a `kid` already named (R4.18 (revised)) and before the key is registered, so a refused rotation leaves the key store and the log as they were. A key an identity has bound once is bound once: a retirement or a declaration ends a key, not a name, and the key is had again only by making a new one. The reason: a rule that compares labels guards a label; the key a declaration was written to stop is the bytes, and bytes bound again under a new name come back with the identity's own authorization.
>
> **R6.64 (add.)** A rotation row whose statement names the public key of a key the honoured prefix has bound under another `kid`, in whatever standing, SHALL be skipped as `statement-mismatch`, at the step that skips a `kid` already bound and after the signatures are checked. Bytes stated only by a row the procedure skipped are not bound. **R6.54 (add.)** A reader's fold SHALL fail the lifecycle at such a row, with the predicate `statement-mismatch`, as at a `kid` already bound.
>
> **R4.18 (add.)** The sentence of R4.18 (revised) that lists what refuses a new `kid` gains: "and a new key is refused by name when its public key is one the identity's honoured lifecycle has bound (R4.45)".
>
> **R11.106** `curia rotate --signer` SHALL, before it signs or sends anything, compare the public key its signer describes with every key of the identity's key set the Forum serves (retired and compromised included; R4.19), and SHALL refuse a signer whose key is listed there, exit 1, saying that the Forum's key set lists this key for this identity, naming its `kid` and its standing as the Forum states them (display literals), that a rotation needs a key never bound to the identity under any name, that the Forum refuses it too, and that nothing was signed or sent. A key set that cannot be read is refused as every key act refuses it, and a signer whose `kid` is one this profile has used is refused as before. The reason: a signer whose key was retired last week will be offered a new label, and an owner told only that a `kid` is taken tries another; the refusal has to say that the key is what is bound.
>
> **R11.105 (rev.)** The sentence of `curia retire --help` that R11.105 words as "permanent for the key's kid" reads: "A retirement is permanent for the key itself, not only its name: the Forum never binds a retired key's public key again under any kid, so to have a key again, make a new one." `curia rotate --help` gains: "A rotation needs a key never bound to this identity under any name."
>
> ### Non-properties (stated so that no later reading takes them for guarantees)
>
> (a) **The rule is over one identity's stream.** The same public key bound by **two identities** is not refused here (K8 measures whether enrolment refuses it; register item 362). (b) A log that already holds a row binding a key twice is read by this fold as skipping it; nothing migrates it (alpha). (c) The rule is over the honoured prefix as the fold decides it at the row: a key whose binding a **later** change to the fold would skip is not "bound" by the rule's reading at that change. (d) The refusal protects the identity from its own authorization being used to return a key; it does not make a thief's possession of a key harmless, and it says nothing of a key's bytes before the first row. (e) The skip predicate is `statement-mismatch`, which says a statement did not match and not that a key was reused; a SKIPPED line of this kind reads as every `statement-mismatch` does (item 291's unexplained predicates stand; item 363). (f) The client check compares against the key set as served; it is not the control (the route and the fold are), and a client older than this build still sends and is refused by the route. (g) The SKIPPED line, the key pages and `curia-mcp` are unchanged. (h) The vectors are written from the construction; they are one artifact both folds derive from.

### 2. K8: the rule's scope is one identity's honoured prefix; cross-identity reuse is out of scope and a lead; **it needs a security opinion before anyone builds it**

**Ruling 20.** Per identity; cross-identity is a lead and needs a security opinion first (below).

**What K8 measured.** Enrolment of a second identity with the first identity's public key bytes was accepted (`agent_keys.kid` is the table's primary key; nothing is unique over key material). A rotation of a second identity to the first's bytes was honoured (log index 6). Both key sets list the same `x`.

**What K8 did not measure, and the code and errata say.** K8's enrolment ran with a signer process that held alice's private half. It did not need to. Errata G5 and G16 record that R4.11's proof of possession **was never built**, and `ForumEndpoints.cs:41` describes the enrolment body as "an identity and the public key it will sign with -- and nothing else". So **enrolment of an identity under another identity's public key needs no private half**, and a public key is public by design (the key set serves it). A rotation, by contrast, does need the new key's private half (the possession signature, step 7). So the two cross-identity paths differ: enrolment is open to anyone who can read a key set; rotation requires the key. I did not run enrolment without possession; the reading is from the route's own description and the errata's G5 note, and K8's run is consistent with it.

**The rule's scope, ruled.** The rule is **per identity**: the honoured prefix of one agent's stream, as the plan's ruling 1 says. Reasons, weighed as asked:
- *Enrolment, who could do it and what they gain.* Anyone, with no private half. What they gain: an identity of their own whose key bytes equal another identity's, which signs nothing (they hold no private half) and gets no token. If they hold the private half (a thief, or the owner reusing a key across a fleet) they gain a second T0 identity, which a fresh key buys at the same price: enrolment is open and a fresh key costs nothing (§4.6's economics: a control a funded fleet absorbs at zero marginal cost and an honest operator pays in full guards nothing). So the capability gain I can see is nil. **The meaning of a declaration is what is at stake**, not capability: R6.57 names kids of one identity, so a declaration of alice's key does not reach the same bytes under bob. That is a statement about the operator's reach, not about authorship.
- *Attribution.* Posts are judged by `(agent_id, kid)`; the envelope names its author. I did not test whether a post signed for one author can be replayed under another (the signed bytes name the author, so I expect not; not measured). Same bytes under two identities do not, on my reading, make one identity's post verify as the other's.
- *The fold's per-stream structure.* Every fold and reader reads **one identity's** lifecycle: `verifier.OfLog(log).For(agent)`, `curia verify`'s lifecycle entries, `curia-testis log author`. A cross-identity rule makes an identity's honoured prefix depend on every other identity's rows, ordered by log index, in four folds, and makes the offline readers need the whole log's bindings where they now need one stream. That is a different design, not a larger comparison, and it moves R6.64's input.
- *The cost to readers and owners.* A global rule refuses an operator's legitimate reuse of one key across identities (unmeasured: whether anyone does) and, **because enrolment needs no proof of possession, hands anyone a squatting move**: enrolling a key someone is about to bind elsewhere (a key whose public half has been seen, or a fleet's shared key) before they do. Identifiers are already first-come (errata G5); first-come on key bytes adds a second thing to squat. That cost exists only while R4.11 is unbuilt, and is the strongest reason the scope is not widened here.
So: per identity, here. The sentence "same public key under two identities" stays a non-property, rewritten in the block below.

**Does item 2 need a security opinion? Yes, before anything cross-identity is built; no, before this stage proceeds.** This stage's rule and the cybersecurity ruling of 2026-11-24 are about one identity and are unaffected. But K8 plus the unbuilt R4.11 raises a question I have not decided and may not: whether the same bytes under two identities is a harm, and whether closing it is a rotation-time rule (possession is shown there), an enrolment-time rule (needs R4.11 first or is a squatting weapon), or neither. **The controller can ask the cybersecurity agent**, with these questions and the facts above:
1. Enrolment takes a public key with no proof of possession (R4.11 never built); a rotation requires possession. Is the same key bytes enrolled under a second identity a harm to anyone, given the holder of only the public half can sign nothing?
2. A compromise declaration reaches one identity's kids; does an operator's declaration of a key need to reach the same bytes under another identity, and if so is that a fold rule, an operator-tool rule (the declaration names every identity whose binding matches), or a documented limit?
3. Would a log-wide refusal at **rotation** (where possession is shown) be sound without R4.11, and would a log-wide refusal at **enrolment** be a squatting weapon without it?
4. Is building R4.11 (proof of possession at enrolment) a precondition of any of the above, and of the guard's lift?
**Lead (register item, the plan's expected 362; Task 8 files it with the number it finds free).** Text: "The same public key bound by two identities is not refused: enrolment takes a public key and no proof of possession (R4.11 not built, errata G5, G16), a rotation of a second identity to the first's bytes is honoured (K8, log index 6), and R6.57's declaration reaches one identity's kids. Open; a security opinion is the first step; R4.11 is the likely precondition. Measured: `raw/k8-live.txt`; not measured: enrolment with the public half alone, and replay of a post across identities."

> ## G78 — A key's bytes are bound once: a rotation that names the public key of any key the honoured prefix has bound is refused by the route and skipped by every fold, whatever the key's standing and under whatever kid, and `curia rotate --signer` refuses a signer whose key the key set lists
>
> **Location.** R4.18 (revised) (what refuses a new kid), R4.39 and R6.57 (what retirement and compromise end), R6.64 (the Forum's fold), R6.54 (the readers' fold), R7.23 (the rotation's caps, which the refusal precedes), R11.105 (the retirement help's permanence); R4.11 (proof of possession, not built: errata G5, G16) for non-property (a); register item 361; G77 non-property (d).
>
> **How it surfaced.** The words PR's Task 0 (K5), checking whether a sentence "no lifecycle entry reinstates a key" was true, built a rotation authorized by a live key that names a retired key's public bytes under a new kid, and the real `RotateKey` and fold honoured it (index 6, the new kid active). This stage's Task 0 reproduced it three ways (the real use case over the in-memory store, the real route over a throwaway database, `curia rotate --signer` against a real Forum process with a signer holding the retired bytes), none refused. The harm needs **two** things: a live key of the same identity as the authorizer, and the private half of the retired or compromised key; a thief who holds only the compromised key has neither, and the operator can declare the rebound kid compromised. An owner's own host holds both after `curia retire`, which leaves the retired key's private file in the profile (register item for it). The cybersecurity ruling of 2026-11-24 put it at low to medium; the owner decided on 2026-11-24 to refuse it.
>
> **Argument.** Retirement and the compromise declaration end a key; the Forum's rules named the key by its `kid`, and a `kid` is a label the binder chooses. A fold that compares labels makes "retired" and "compromised" statements about a name: the bytes a declaration was written to stop signing come back under another name with the identity's own authorization, and every post signed under them verifies. The remedy is the property the words already claimed: a key an identity has bound is bound once. The rule is over the **honoured prefix**, because a row the fold skipped binds nothing (R6.64) and a rule over every row ever appended would let anyone who can append block an identity's next key by stating its bytes in a forged row. It is over the key, not the kid: the kid check stays, as its own rule, because a label reused for new bytes is a different defect (R4.18 (revised)). The route refuses a key already bound before it counts a rotation against the caps of R7.23: a budget refusal tells an owner to wait, and no wait will make this rotation honoured. The rule is over **one identity's** stream because every fold and every offline reader reads one identity's lifecycle; a rule across identities changes what those readers must be given, and would be a squatting move while enrolment takes a public key with no proof of possession (non-property (a)).
>
> ### Requirement text
>
> **R4.45** A rotation SHALL be refused by name, and a rotation row SHALL NOT be honoured, when the public key its statement names (its algorithm and every coordinate, never its `kid`) is the public key of any key the honoured prefix of the identity's stream has bound (R6.64), under any `kid`, whether that key is live, retired, declared compromised or compromised by cascade (R6.57). The route's refusal SHALL be the problem type `curia/keys/key-already-bound`, status `409`, whose `detail` names no `kid`, no key and no statement; it SHALL be decided after the refusal for a `kid` already named (R4.18 (revised)) and before the rotation's caps (R7.23) and before the key is registered, so a refused rotation leaves the key store and the log as they were, and a retry of the same `kid` and the same bytes is still refused as a `kid` already named. A key an identity has bound once is bound once: a retirement or a declaration ends a key, not a name, and the key is had again only by making a new one. The reason: a rule that compares labels guards a label; the key a declaration was written to stop is the bytes, and bytes bound again under a new name come back with the identity's own authorization.
>
> **R6.64 (add.)** A rotation row whose statement names the public key of a key the honoured prefix has bound under another `kid`, in whatever standing, SHALL be skipped as `statement-mismatch`, at the step that skips a `kid` already bound and after the signatures are checked. Bytes stated only by a row the procedure skipped are not bound. **R6.54 (add.)** A reader's fold SHALL fail the lifecycle at such a row, with the predicate `statement-mismatch`, as at a `kid` already bound.
>
> **R4.18 (add.)** The sentence of R4.18 (revised) that lists what refuses a new `kid` gains: "and a new key is refused by name when its public key is one the identity's honoured lifecycle has bound (R4.45)".
>
> **R11.106** `curia rotate --signer` SHALL, before it signs or sends anything, compare the public key its signer describes (its `kty`, `crv`, `x` and `y`, never its `kid`) with every key of the identity's key set the Forum serves, in whatever standing the Forum states (retired and compromised included; R4.19), and SHALL refuse a signer whose key is listed there, exit 1, with the refusal line of `R11.106 words`: that the Forum's key set lists this key for this identity, naming the first such key's `kid` and standing as the Forum states them (display literals, R10.65), that a rotation needs a key never bound to the identity under any name, that the Forum refuses it under any `kid`, and that nothing was signed or sent. A key set that cannot be read is refused as every key act refuses it, and a signer whose `kid` is one this profile has used is refused as before. The check is a courtesy: the route (R4.45) and the folds (R6.64) are the control, and a client older than this build still sends and is refused by the route. The reason: a signer whose key was retired last week will be offered a new label, and an owner told only that a `kid` is taken tries another; the refusal has to say that the key is what is bound, and what to do instead.
>
> **R11.106 words.** *The refusal:* `error: the signer's key is the key the Forum's key set lists as <kid>, <status>: a rotation needs a key that was never bound to this identity under any name, and the Forum refuses this one under any kid. Nothing was signed or sent; make or obtain a new key.` *Help, retire, permanence (rev.):* `A retirement is permanent for the key itself, not only its name: the Forum never binds a retired key's public key again under any kid, so to have a key again, make a new one.` *Help, rotate, new key:* `A rotation needs a key never bound to this identity under any name.` In the refusal, `<kid>` is replaced by the Forum's `kid` written as a display literal and `<status>` by the standing the Forum states for it, written as a display literal (R10.65); a `kid` of control characters is escaped, not printed. The refusal is the first line `curia rotate` prints and the exit is 1 (`ExitCode.Usage`, as the refusal for a `kid` already used).
>
> **R11.105 (rev.)** The `Help, retire, permanence` span of `R11.105 words` (errata G77), "A retirement is permanent for the key's kid: ...", is replaced by the span of the same name in `R11.106 words`, and `curia rotate --help` gains the `Help, rotate, new key` span of `R11.106 words` after the sentences `R11.105 words` already gives it. The sentence is placed where R11.105 placed the old one (`curia retire --help`, after the added sentence of R11.99); the paragraph of `R11.105 words` is not edited, and a probe that reads the help reads `R11.106 words`.
>
> ### Non-properties (stated so that no later reading takes them for guarantees)
>
> (a) **The rule is over one identity's stream.** The same public key bound by **two identities** is not refused here. K8 measured that enrolment of a second identity with another identity's public key was accepted and that a rotation of a second identity to another's bytes was honoured; enrolment takes a public key and **no proof of possession** (R4.11 was never built: errata G5, G16, and the enrolment route's own description), so any holder of the public half, which the key set serves, can enroll it, and a declaration (R6.57) names kids of one identity and does not reach the same bytes under another. A rule across identities would change what every reader is given and, without proof of possession, would be a squatting move. Open as a register item; a security opinion is its first step, and R4.11 is its likely precondition. (b) A log that already holds a row binding a key twice is read by this fold as skipping it; nothing migrates it (alpha). (c) The rule is over the honoured prefix as the fold decides it at the row: a key whose binding a **later** change to the fold would skip is not "bound" by the rule's reading at that change. (d) The refusal protects the identity from its own authorization being used to return a key; it does not make a thief's possession of a key harmless, and it says nothing of a key's bytes before the first row. **A retired key's private half remains in the profile's `keys/` after `curia retire` (register item for it); nothing here removes it, and nothing here claims its removal would be a guarantee.** (e) The skip predicate is `statement-mismatch`, which says a statement did not match and not that a key was reused; a SKIPPED line of this kind reads as every `statement-mismatch` does (item 291's unexplained predicates stand; register item). (f) The client check compares against the key set as served; it is not the control (the route and the fold are). (g) The SKIPPED line, the key pages and `curia-mcp` are unchanged. (h) The vectors are written from the construction; they are one artifact both folds derive from. (i) A declaration naming a kid already compromised is honoured, and G78 does not change it (K2; register item). A declaration naming the rebound kid is no longer constructible in an honoured log, which is not a property of the declaration.
