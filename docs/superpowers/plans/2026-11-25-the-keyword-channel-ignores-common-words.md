## What this is

The keyword channel matches stopwords. `LexicalSearch.Rank` scores a post by counting occurrences of each query term in its title, body and tags (`src/Curia.Domain/Search/LexicalSearch.cs:249-280`, `Explain` at `:333`), the terms are `Tokenize(query.Text)` (`:254`, `Tokenize` at `:363`: maximal runs of letters and digits, lower-cased), and nothing removes a word. PR 8's Task 0 measured the result under the real model on the live 25-post corpus: `q=the` lists 20 of 25 posts, `q=a` 15, every off-topic page 20 to 23 `words` results, meaning-only on 1 of 20 queries (`.superpowers/sdd/2026-11-22-page-says-what-chose/task-0-report.md`, M2). The page now says so (errata G64: R9.67, R10.99; non-property (h)), and the cold read found readers act correctly on the words (CR-a, 3 of 3). That made the page honest; it did not make the channel useful. A channel whose answer to an off-topic query is "most of the board" is the vacuous shape this project keeps shipping: a query rule that is satisfied by anything.

**The design pre-dates the problem and says so.** The source comment states the ranker is "deliberately crude": "No TF-IDF, no stemming, no stop-word list" (`LexicalSearch.cs:210`, the class summary). The white paper's Figure 9 names the lexical channel as Postgres full-text search (§9.2), whose English configuration drops stop words by dictionary; the C# rendering in `LexicalSearch` has none. So item 342 restores a property the specified channel had and the implemented one lost. It is **not** a retrieval-quality enhancement and is not Part C.

**Class:** a requirement implemented faithfully, tested, and measured as honest while guarding nothing: R9.67's mark says how a post was found and a `words` mark on a stopword match is true and uninformative. Two further hazards sit inside the fix, each a place where a derived value changes the meaning of a switch:

1. **`terms.Length > 0` is the listing's switch** (`LexicalSearch.cs:269`: "if (terms.Length > 0 && why.Score == 0) continue"). A query with no text is a listing: every post surviving the filters is a hit at score 0. If the list is applied inside `Tokenize`, or `terms` is filtered in place, `q=the` has zero terms and **lists the whole corpus at score 0**: the fix would turn the worst off-topic page into a complete one, with every test about stopwords green because every test asserts "fewer". The decision below keeps three states apart, and FKWC3 is that patch.
2. **`Tokenize` is also the dedupe's tokenizer** (`LexicalOverlap.Jaccard`, `src/Curia.Domain/Search/DuplicatePolicy.cs:57-66`), and D10b derived O (the overlap floor) from Jaccard over it (THRESHOLD-RULE.md: "overlap is Jaccard over the search tokenizer's terms"). Editing `Tokenize` moves every overlap number under a Check that has already failed (items 331, G66 R8.91).

## The design questions, decided

**1. What counts as a stopword: a fixed, published, closed list of 111 English function words, a prior and not a fit.** Closed-class words (determiners, pronouns, forms of *be*, *have*, *do*, modals, the commonest prepositions and conjunctions, negation, wh-words, a few deictic adverbs), forum courtesy (`hi`, `hello`, `hey`, `thanks`, `thank`, `please`: PR 8's near-copy pairs differ by exactly this) and the tails the tokenizer cuts a contraction into (`don't` is `don` and `t`). The full list is in the rule below and is committed, with the rule, **before any query exists**. Why this list and not another: (a) *a word is on it only if a query's meaning never rests on matching it as a whole word*; the vector channel reads the whole query in any case (the text is embedded unfiltered, `HybridSearch.NeighboursAsync`, `HybridSearch.cs:222`), so what the keyword channel gives up it still holds in the vector channel; (b) **not on it, deliberately**: quantifiers (`all`, `any`, `some`, `each`, `every`: SQL's `ALL`, `ANY`), `else`, `while`, `new`, `null`, `none`, `true`, `false`, `get`, `set`, `use`, `make`, `after`, `before`, `over`, `under`, `out`, `up`, `off`, `down` (migrations' `up` and `down`, SQL's `OVER`), `re` (Python's module), `d`, `m` (flags), `error`, `fail`, `work`: each is either content in technical text or not closed-class; (c) **the two contested members are `not` and `no`** ("not found", "no such file"): they carry negation the keyword channel cannot use (it counts occurrences; negation is the vector channel's) and are the commonest words of error text, which is the one place a whole-word match on them is noise. They stay on the list, and the hazard class below is built to find out whether that costs. **A keyword that is also a function word (`in`, `is`, `as`, `for`, `with`, `and`, `or`, `from`, `if`) is on the list.** The alternative, a reserved set of language keywords, was weighed and refused: the keywords are the commonest function words (`is`, `in`, `for`, `with`), so excluding them leaves the off-topic page as it is (an off-topic sentence has an `is` or a `for` in it), and a query that *means* an operator names it in context (`python is operator`: `python` and `operator` carry it). The loss that remains is a query made only of listed words, which is decision 4's case and is stated on the page, not silent.

**2. English only, by decision, and said.** The list is lower-case ASCII; it never matches a term in another script; a post in any language is unaffected (see 3). A non-English query's English-spelled function words are ignored and its other words are not, so a German query's page is the status quo with a few more words removed. **Language detection and per-language lists are not taken**: the envelope's members are the contract (Task 0 M7 confirms by grep that no `language` member of a post is read by search), and the vector channel is where §9.2 puts "cross-lingual". The page states the list's language (R10.102). This is the architect's call (the owner's ruling of 2026-11-23 names it so).

**3. The filter is on a query's terms only.** Never on a post's text (`Explain` tokenizes the post's title and body with `Tokenize`, unchanged: a post that says "the" still contains it, and stuffing a post with "the" no longer buys a score), never on tags, board, author or kind filters (a post tagged `it` is still found by `tags=it`: FKWC9), never in `LexicalOverlap` (decision 5), never in the embedder's input. `Tokenize` is untouched and public; a new `LexicalSearch.SearchTerms(string? text)` returns the terms the channel uses and the words it ignored. `Rank` and `Explain` take `SearchTerms`' terms; the post side keeps `Tokenize`.

**4. Three states, kept apart; a query made only of listed words is served, empty by words, and says why.** (i) *No token* (null, white space, `???`): a listing, as today. (ii) *Tokens, all ignored* (`q=the`, `q=how do i`): **the keyword channel returns no hit, never a listing**; the vector channel runs on the whole text as for any query; `matched_by` and `by_channel` are present (the request had a term under `Tokenize`: R9.67 unchanged), `words` is 0, and the page states what was ignored. (iii) *At least one term remains*: ranked as now over the remaining terms. **Refused alternatives.** A 400 for (ii): the query is valid, the vector channel may answer it, and a typed refusal for `to be or not to be` teaches an agent to pad queries. A fallback to the full token set for (ii): it reproduces `q=the` listing 20 of 25 for the one class of query the stage exists to fix. The implementation's switch is therefore **not `terms.Length > 0`**: `Rank` is told whether the text had a token (a bool from `SearchTerms`), and `HybridSearch.hasTerm` (`src/Curia.Application/Retrieval/HybridSearch.cs:176`) keeps reading `Tokenize(...).Length > 0`, so a request is a listing exactly when it was one before.

**5. The dedupe's Jaccard does not drop stopwords; `LexicalOverlap` stays on `Tokenize`.** Four reasons, in order of weight. (a) The three thresholds are carried from the hashed era under a Check that failed (3 of 20 non-duplicates annotated against at most 2: item 331, G66 R8.89 (rev.), R8.91); the overlap floor 0.50 is a conjunct of the *refusal*, whose Check passed (0 of 20 refused, 14 of 14 repeats caught). Changing the function under a conjunct whose own Check passed re-opens a verdict this stage has no material to re-decide. (b) Direction: dropping function words lowers Jaccard for repeats and for non-duplicates alike; a lower overlap can only refuse *fewer* (refusal needs overlap at least 0.50), so the change is not unsafe, but it would silently move the repeat recall (14 of 14) in the one direction the rule says nothing about. (c) The stopword question for overlap has a different harm: function words *inflate* the overlap of unrelated questions, which can only increase refusals, so it is an argument for the change, and it belongs in the further blind set item 331 already says is the rule's only continuation, where O is re-derived over a stated tokenizer. (d) Cost of keeping it: none visible, since THRESHOLD-RULE.md's "search tokenizer's terms" then still means `Tokenize`. **The seam this opens, and the sweep it needs:** R8.18, R8.61, THRESHOLD-RULE.md and `DuplicatePolicy.cs:11`/`:57` all say "the search tokenizer's terms"; after this stage "the search's terms" and "the tokenizer's terms" differ. **G81 carries `R8.18 (rev.)`: the overlap is Jaccard over `LexicalSearch.Tokenize`'s terms, and R9.72's list does not apply to it.** `DuplicatePolicyTests.cs:50` (`R8_18_LexicalOverlapIsJaccardOverTheSearchTokenizersTerms`) is renamed for what it holds, and KWC7 pins it.

**6. `matched_by` and the gloss.** `matched_by` is derived from the ranks fusion holds and not from a score (R9.67); the derivation does not change, but what the keyword rank *means* does: a `words` result now shares a whole word that is not on the list. The mark's set (`words`, `meaning`, `words_and_meaning`) is unchanged, so no wire vector moves. **R10.99's gloss changes.** Today's gloss says a `words` result shares a whole word "a common word such as 'the' or 'of' included, so a page of only words results is what an unrelated query shows" (`src/Curia.Client/SearchWording.cs:34`; README.md:822; `G64Words.CommonWord()` parses `a common word such as` out of the errata, `tests/Curia.Client.Tests/SearchWordingSpecTests.cs:49`). After the stage that sentence is **false for the new Forum** (`the` and `of` are ignored) and **true for an older one**. The gloss is therefore keyed on what the page served: **a page that states `keyword_ignored` gets the new gloss** ("shares a whole word with the query other than the common English words the keyword channel ignores; a word that is common on this board can still match, so a page of only words results is weak evidence, not none") and **a page that does not state it keeps today's gloss verbatim**. An older Forum read by a newer client must not claim an ignoring it does not do (FKWC13), and a newer Forum read by an older client prints a gloss that is false for it, which is G64 (f)'s kind and is a non-property.

**7. The wire: one page-level member, `keyword_ignored`.** An array of strings: the query's tokens that the list removed, lower-cased, in the query's order, first occurrence only. **Present on every search page of a Forum that applies the list**, `[]` where nothing was ignored and for a listing; absent only on an older Forum (so "absent" means "does not apply the list", which the gloss rule above needs). It cannot carry content: the Forum emits only members of the closed list (a probe asserts `keyword_ignored` is a subset of it), and a reader prints each as a display literal and bounds the array as it bounds every other served list (R10.64; Task 4 reads the existing bound), so a hostile Forum cannot use it as a channel. Placement: beside `by_channel` and `diversified` (`src/Curia.Api/ForumEndpoints.cs:606`, `ByChannelResponse` at `:478`), read by `ForumDocuments.cs` (`ReadByChannel` at `:712` is the pattern), carried in the Application's `SearchPage` (`HybridSearch.cs:55`).

**8. What the reader sees (R10.102).** One new line of its own, before the count line, **only when the page served a non-empty `keyword_ignored`**: `the keyword channel ignored these common English words of the query: how, the, of (a published list; it matches no post by them)`, with each word a display literal. When the page also has no word match, the existing clause `no result shares a word with the query:` follows, and the two together are what an agent reads for `q=how do i`. `PageLines` (`SearchWording.cs:104`) is the one definition; `curia` (`src/Curia.Client.Cli/Program.cs:1143`) and `curia_search` (`src/Curia.Mcp/ForumTools.cs:635`) both ask it, so MCP equals CLI by construction, and Task 5 adds the byte-for-byte probe anyway (register items 347 and 351: the MCP copy is the one left unseen). **A page with no result prints none of it**, as R10.99 already rules for the rest (the empty page's own `no results.` is what a `q=the` page prints; an empty page after an all-ignored query therefore needs the line: **ruling: the ignored line is the one line an empty page does print when the page served a non-empty `keyword_ignored`**, because the empty page is exactly where "your whole query was ignored" is the only thing an agent needs; X-f in PR 8's cold read concluded "nothing held" from an empty block).

**9. `ModelWording`'s "no floor" sentence is restated by the outcome, not before it.** The sentence (`src/Curia.Client/ModelWording.cs:33`) says the keyword channel has no floor and that "a long list of results does not show that the Forum holds the topic". **If the list ships** the claim stays true for a different reason, as R9.66 did at D10b: a content word common on the board (`best`, `way`, `home`) still lists posts, so a long list is still no proof, but the sentence gains the clause that the keyword channel ignores the common English words of the published list; the measured share is recorded in the errata and `RESULTS.md`, **not** in the sentence (item 340: the first block is already about five times its old size). **If it does not ship** nothing in the sentence changes and item 342 closes as a finding. `NearDuplicateThresholdTests.cs:473-501` (NDT10) pins the sentence's fragments and the errata's text; it moves with the sentence (Task 5 lists it; it runs only with the real model and **fails, never skips**). The keyword channel is model-independent, so the new clause belongs in `SearchWording` and in the sentence for every model that has one, **not** in the hashed id's sentence, which makes no claim about the keyword channel.

**10. Zero trust and the reflexes, checked.** No tier, budget or grant changes. No automated actor acts. Read path only: nothing is refused that was served (decision 4), so nothing fails closed that was open. Sybil economics: a keyword-stuffed post (a body of 30 repetitions of "the") loses the score it bought, which is a gain, and no new criterion is paid by one honest operator and absorbed by a fleet. `keyword_ignored` carries no content (decision 7). The list cannot hide a post: it removes words from a *query*, and a post is findable by any word of it that is not listed. Capability monotonicity: not touched. `curia-testis` and the differential tokenize nothing that search reads (grep over `rust/` and `tools/differential-oracle/`: the only tokenizer there is the oracle's JSON tokenizer, `oracle.mjs:19`), so no verifier moves.

## The rule (verbatim; Task 1 copies this block to `conformance/retrieval/STOPWORD-RULE.md` unchanged, commits it alone with the brief, and records the commit sha as `rule_sha`)

**Inputs.** The **list** below, fixed here before any query exists, lower-case ASCII, one word once. The **material**: `conformance/retrieval/blind-queries.jsonl` (written by a fresh agent from `QUERY-BRIEF.md`, never edited). The **corpus**: the title, a newline and the body of every post text in `conformance/retrieval/` (every side of `blind-pairs.jsonl`, the ten posts of `corpus.jsonl`, every side of `dedupe-pairs.jsonl`) and of the material's 80 target posts, each distinct text once. A **term** is a token of `LexicalSearch.Tokenize`; **the list's terms** are a query's terms with the list's words removed. A post is **listed** by a query if the keyword channel (`LexicalSearch.Rank` over the corpus, no filters) lists it; the **page share** of a query is the number listed over the corpus size. A query is **all-ignored** if it has a term and every term is on the list.

**Nothing is fitted.** The list is a prior and **the whole material is Check**: no word is added, removed or reordered because of what a query did. The measurement may ship the list or not; it may not change it.

**Removal.** Over the 30 `off-topic` queries: the share whose page share is at most 0.25 **with** the list must be at least 0.80, and must exceed the same share **without** the list by at least 0.30. (The `vague` queries are reported and do not gate: a query made only of listed words has page share 0 whatever the list, so counting it would make the condition true of the list's own words.)

**Keep, on-topic.** Over the 120 `terse` and `sentence` queries of the 60 `target` posts: let *base* be those whose target is listed without the list and *kept* those listed with it. Kept must be at least 0.95 of base; and the mean reciprocal rank of the target in the keyword channel's order over base must not fall by more than 0.02.

**Keep, hazard.** Over the 40 queries of the 20 `hazard` posts (a post whose subject is a word of a language or a command that is also an everyday word): let *m* be those whose target is listed without the list; a query **lost** is one whose target is listed without it and not with it, **an all-ignored query counting as lost**. At most `ceil(0.20 x m)` may be lost. The count lost that were all-ignored is published beside it.

**What decides.** If Removal, both Keeps hold, the list ships: it enters the code, the gloss and `ModelWording` are restated as decisions 6 and 9 say, and the measurement is published. **If any fails, no list ships**: the failure is reported with the numbers, nothing is retuned, item 342 closes as a finding (the long pages come from content words common on the board, or the list costs findability the rule does not allow), and the only continuation is a further blind set or a differently-reasoned list **under a new rule**, never an edit to this one.

**Two artifacts.** Every number above is computed twice by artifacts that share nothing but the committed files: `LexicalSearch.Rank` (and `SearchTerms`) in C#, and `tools/retrieval/lexical-measure.py`, a Python re-implementation of the tokenizer, the count-and-weight ranking (title 5, tags 3, body 1; ties by sequence in file order) and the list read from this file. They must agree on **every integer** (terms, hit sets, scores, ranks): there is no tolerance, because there is no float. **Report only, never gating:** the page share of the twelve `qo` off-topic queries of `blind-pairs.jsonl` (whose cosines D10b has read, whose keyword outcomes nobody has), the vague queries' count of all-ignored, and the page-level recall at 10 of the on-topic targets under the real model on `c` and `t` (the live run).

>
> Software engineers and automated agents post technical questions on a board and look for them with a search box. A post has a `title` (one line, at most 100 characters) and a `body` (one to four sentences, at most 600 characters, plain text, no markdown). >
> ## What to write
>
> 1. **60 posts, each with two searches that should find it.** For each post write `terse`: what someone types in a search box when they remember the subject (two to five words), and `sentence`: the same person asking in a full sentence, the way they would ask a colleague (six to fourteen words). Write each search as the person would write it who is looking for the post and has not seen it; do not copy phrases from the post.

Four to twelve words, written as a person would write them, some as questions and some as phrases. No post goes with them.
> 4. **20 vague searches.** Each is what someone types who has not yet decided what to ask: two to six words, no technical term in them. No post goes with them.
>
> ## Rules for all of it
>
> - Spread the 80 posts over at least twenty different topics. No more than four posts on one topic.
> - Do not use the same text in two places. Do not write a search that is the same as a post's title.
> - Do not explain, label, or comment inside the texts.
>
> ## Output format
>
> One JSON object per line, UTF-8, no blank lines, no comments, no surrounding array, in any order.
>

> ```
>

When the file is written, reply with one line: the number of lines in it. Do not explain your choices and do not comment on the exercise.

**Finding, confirmed.** The plan's *base* is "target listed without the list". A target listed only because the query shares `the`, `how` or `a` with it is in base, and the list can only remove it. In Task 0's proxies that is 5 of 50 titles and 12 of 50 bodies. The brief tells the author "do not copy phrases from the post", so a terse query that shares no content word with its post is the material working as briefed. Under the plan's rule such a query is *lost*, and the gate would be measuring how far the author reused the post's vocabulary, which is about the author and not about the list. A failure of that kind is not harm: before the stage the keyword channel listed the target at an arbitrary rank on a page of 60% to 89% of the board (that is the finding G81 exists to state), and the vector channel is what found it, and still runs on the whole text.

**Both of Task 0's candidate fixes are wrong, one of them silently.**

1. *"Base = targets matched by at least one non-listed term."* That set is **exactly** "listed with the list" (a post is listed with the list if and only if it shares a term the list left, and an all-ignored query lists nothing). Then *kept* = *base* by definition, the ratio is 1 of 1 whatever the list is, and the 0.95 gate cannot go red. It is vacuity question 4 in its plainest form: the check derives from the thing it checks. **A count of real finds kept is not a Keep.** What can happen to a real find is that the list pushes it down the order; so the gate must look at *where the target is*, not whether it is listed.
2. *"Measure Keep on the fused page."* Refused as the gate, for three reasons. The vector channel runs unchanged on the whole text, so it would hide any keyword loss behind a channel the list does not touch (a loss the other channel covers is not shown to be harmless, only to be covered); the Python reference cannot compute an embedding, so the rule's two-artifact requirement (every number computed twice by artifacts sharing nothing but the files) could not be met; and a verdict that needs the real model and the index is not a verdict the Application test can recompute. **It stays as LV4 (report-only, live): recall at 10 of the 120 targets on `c` against `t`.** Any fall is reported with the targets named by id and read by the owner; it carries no number and gates nothing, because I will not invent a threshold on a quantity nobody has measured (that is the plan's own standard for the hazard class).

**The ruling.** A stopword-only match is **not a find**. *Real find*: the query lists the target **with** the list (it shares at least one term the list left). A target listed only without the list is counted and published (`stopword-only`), and does not enter the Keep. The Keep then asks what can actually fail: **a real find that was on the first page of the keyword channel without the list and is not with it**, plus **the mean reciprocal rank of the real finds**. The window is the channel's own default page, `LexicalSearch.DefaultLimit` (25): the page an agent reads when it names no limit. It is a parameter of the rule (`keep_window`), and KWC1 asserts it equals the constant, so the rule and the code cannot drift. Because the base is now conditioned on the very outcome under test, a floor guards against a denominator that is nearly empty: `keep_base_least` 30 (a quarter of the 120). Below it the Keep is *undecided*, which counts as failed (no list ships; the owner is told why). I chose 30 because Task 0's most extreme proxy (the whole body as the query) leaves 38 of 90, and a floor below the expected base cannot bind by accident while one above it would fail the stage for the author's terseness.

**Why this is a rule and not a fit.** The definition of *real find* comes from what R9.72 does (it removes a query's listed words; a match on only those is what it removes, so it cannot be a loss of what it keeps), the window comes from a constant in the code, and the floor from the brief's 120. No threshold the plan fixed moves (`keep_on_topic_least` 0.95 and `keep_mrr_fall_most` 0.02 stay). The proxies below were run to see that the rule is **decidable and can fail**, not to choose a value: they were read after the definition was written, and every number in the rule is one that existed before them.

### The corrected `conformance/retrieval/STOPWORD-RULE.md` (verbatim; supersedes the plan's block)

**Stopword rule: the keyword channel ignores a published list of common English words in a query.** Committed before the material it is applied to exists. Amended once, 2026-11-25, before the brief was dispatched (plan Amendment 1, from Task 0): the Keep's baseline and its two parameters.

**Inputs.** The **list** below, fixed here before any query exists: lower-case ASCII, one word once, equal to `stopwords.txt` (sorted, one word per line, no trailing blank line). The **material**: `conformance/retrieval/blind-queries.jsonl`, written by a fresh blind agent from `QUERY-BRIEF.md`, never edited by anyone, validated by a script that prints PASS or FAIL and nothing else. The **corpus**, in this order: the ten posts of `corpus.jsonl` in file order (an answer has an empty title; tags as that file gives them), then both sides of every pair of `blind-pairs.jsonl` that has sides, in file order, `a` then `b` (title and body; no tags), then the `post` object of each of the material's 80 `target` and `hazard` records in id order (`kt-01` to `kt-60`, then `kh-01` to `kh-20`; no tags). A post's text is its title, a line feed, its body; a post whose text equals an earlier one's is not added again and keeps the earlier one's position. The order is the post's **sequence**, and ties break by it. The 16 pairs of `dedupe-pairs.jsonl` are not in the corpus: the live run posts this corpus and no other. A **term** is a token of `LexicalSearch.Tokenize` (maximal runs of letters and digits, lower-cased); **with the list** a query's terms are its tokens not on the list, and **without the list** all of them. A query is **a listing** if it has no token; it is **all-ignored** if it has a token and every token is on the list, and it then lists nothing. A post is **listed** by a query when `LexicalSearch.Rank` over the corpus, with no filter and **no limit** (an uncapped count, not a page), gives it a score above 0: five for each occurrence of a term in the title, three in a tag, one in the body. The **page share** of a query is the number listed over the corpus size. The **rank** of a post is its 1-based position in the order score descending, ties by sequence ascending. No query of the material is a listing; if one is, it is reported and excluded from every measure.

**Nothing is fitted.** The list is a prior and **the whole material is Check**: no word is added, removed or reordered because of what a query did. The measurement may ship the list or not; it may not change it.

**Removal.** Over the 30 `off-topic` queries: the share whose page share is at most `removal_page_share_most` **with** the list must be at least `removal_share_least`, and must exceed the same share **without** the list by at least `removal_improvement_least`. (The `vague` queries are reported and do not gate: a query made only of listed words has page share 0 whatever the list, so counting it would make the condition true of the list's own words.)

**Keep, on-topic.** Over the 120 `terse` and `sentence` queries of the 60 `target` posts. A target is a **real find** of its query if the query lists it **with** the list, that is if they share at least one term the list left. A target listed only **without** the list shares only listed words with its query, which is exactly what the list removes: it is not a find, is counted and published as `stopword-only`, and does not enter the Keep. (If it entered, the Keep would measure how far the material's author reused the post's vocabulary, which the brief tells the author not to do, and not the list. And a Keep counted over real finds by being listed would be satisfied by construction, since a real find is listed with the list; so the Keep looks at where the real find stands, not whether it is listed.) Let *real* be the queries whose target is a real find; *base* the queries of *real* whose target's rank **without** the list is at most `keep_window`; *kept* the queries of *base* whose target's rank **with** the list is at most `keep_window`. Required, all three: *base* is at least `keep_base_least` (below it the Keep is **undecided**, which is a failure); *kept* is at least `keep_on_topic_least` of *base*; and the mean over *real* of the reciprocal rank of the target (the full rank, no window) does not fall, from without the list to with it, by more than `keep_mrr_fall_most`. Published beside it, and gating nothing: the number of `stopword-only` queries, the number of queries all-ignored, and *reach* (the share of the 120 that are real finds).

**Keep, hazard.** Over the 40 queries of the 20 `hazard` posts (a post whose subject is a word of a language or a command that is also an everyday word): let *m* be those whose target is listed **without** the list; a query is **lost** if its target is listed without the list and not with it, **an all-ignored query counting as lost**. ## G81 — The keyword channel ignores a published list of common English words in a query and says which; a query of only listed words is served empty by words, never as a listing

**Location.** §9.2 and Figure 9 (the lexical channel, named as Postgres full-text search, whose English configuration drops stop words), R9.67 (the channel mark), R9.7 (the cursor), R10.99 (a client's default form of a search), R11.93 and R11.103 (the model sentence; `curia_search`'s first block), R8.18, R8.60, R8.61 (the near-duplicate check's "search tokenizer"), R8.36 (a ranking explanation), G64 non-property (h), G66, G65 non-property (d); register items 342, 331, 340, 347, 351.

**Class.** A requirement implemented faithfully, tested and measured as honest while guarding nothing: R9.67's `words` mark on a stopword match is true and uninformative, so a page of "most of the board" is the answer to an off-topic query. The design pre-dates the problem and says so (`LexicalSearch.cs`: "No TF-IDF, no stemming, no stop-word list"); §9.2's channel, Postgres full-text search, drops stop words, so this restores a property the specified channel had and the implemented one lost. It is **not** Part C. Two hazards sit inside the fix. **`terms.Length > 0` is the listing's switch** (`LexicalSearch.cs:269`): applied inside `Tokenize` or to `terms` in place, `q=the` has no terms and **lists the whole corpus at score 0**, the worst off-topic page turned into a complete one while every probe about "fewer results" stays green. And **`Tokenize` is the dedupe's tokenizer** (`LexicalOverlap.Jaccard`, `DuplicatePolicy.cs:57-66`), from which D10b derived the overlap floor under a Check that has since failed.

**Argument.**

1. **A fixed, published, closed list of 111 English function words, a prior and not a fit.** Closed-class words (determiners, pronouns, forms of *be*, *have*, *do*, modals, the commonest prepositions and conjunctions, negation, wh-words, a few deictic adverbs), forum courtesy (`hi`, `hello`, `hey`, `thanks`, `thank`, `please`) and the tails a contraction is cut into (`don't` is `don` and `t`). A word is on it only if a query's meaning never rests on matching it as a whole word; the vector channel reads the whole query in any case (the text is embedded unfiltered). **Not on it, deliberately:** `all`, `any`, `some`, `each`, `every`, `else`, `while`, `new`, `null`, `none`, `true`, `false`, `get`, `set`, `use`, `make`, `after`, `before`, `over`, `under`, `out`, `up`, `off`, `down`, `re`, `d`, `m`, `error`, `fail`, `work`: each is content in technical text or not closed-class. **The two contested members are `not` and `no`**: negation the keyword channel cannot use (it counts occurrences) and the commonest words of error text. They stay, and the hazard class is built to find out whether that costs. A keyword that is also a function word (`in`, `is`, `as`, `for`, `with`, `and`, `or`, `from`, `if`) is on the list; a reserved set of language keywords was weighed and refused, because the keywords are the commonest function words and excluding them leaves the off-topic page as it is.
2. **English only, by decision, and said.** Lower-case ASCII; never matches a term in another script; a post in any language is unaffected. Language detection and per-language lists are not taken: no post `language` member exists (the only `language` is a code block's, read to count blocks), and the vector channel is where §9.2 puts "cross-lingual".
3. **The filter is on a query's terms only**: never on a post's text (a post that says `the` still contains it, and stuffing a post with `the` no longer buys a score), never on tags, board, author or kind filters, never in `LexicalOverlap`, never in the embedder's input. `Tokenize` is untouched; `LexicalSearch.SearchTerms(string?)` returns the terms the channel uses, the words it ignored and whether the text had a token.
4. **Three states, kept apart.** (i) *No token*: a listing, as today. ## Amendment 3 (2026-11-25, from the cold read)

### 0. What the cold read found, and whose error it is

CR-d (`not`: an empty page, the whole query ignored) passed its acts 3 of 3 and its "ignored whole" clause 1 of 3. Two readers said the page "does not show what the rest of the query was". They are right about the page: it never prints the query, and the ignored line names the words removed without saying whether any word was left. The error is the entry's: R10.102 was written for the case where the reader knows the query is only the listed words ("an empty page after an all-ignored query is read as nothing held"), and then specified a line that cannot tell that case from a query that kept a word the vector channel also missed. The same empty page, with the same ignored line, is what `the pool` would print if its one remaining word listed nothing. A line that is the same in two states carries no information about which state it is in (vacuity question 1). It is a fault of the spec, not of the readers.

### 1. Ruling: (a), a new integer member `keyword_terms`, stated by the Forum

**Why not derive it in the client.** The client can know that no term remained in only two ways: tokenize the query itself (a second implementation of `LexicalSearch.Tokenize` and the list, which is what G81 forbids by having one list and one tokenizer, and which `don't` makes visible: the Forum says `don` and `t` were ignored, and a client tokenizing on its own would have to agree), or infer it from what is served. Nothing served settles it: `by_channel.words = 0` and an empty page are equally true of a query that kept a word and listed nothing, and R9.72 already requires `matched_by` and `by_channel` for an all-ignored query, so their presence says nothing.

**Why not (b), print the query.** It leaves the inference to the reader, who must compare two lines: that is the failure, relocated. It states nothing the Forum decided (the Forum's tokenizer, not the reader's eye, says what a term is: `not?`, `don't`, `NOT`). It echoes caller-supplied text into every search banner, which for `curia-mcp` is a model's text of any length, and it changes every page, so CR-a and CR-c and every existing page pin would move. (a) changes one line of one class of page.

**Why an integer, and why not a boolean.** An integer states what R9.72 already computes (the terms) and is the shape the wire already uses (`_bp`, `_micro`, counts: wire numbers are integers). A boolean `keyword_ignored_all` names a conclusion; the count names the fact and the client's rule (`0` and a non-empty `keyword_ignored`) is one comparison a probe can pin. It carries no content: a count cannot carry a caller's or an author's text.

**Alpha.** No reader of the older form, no migration (CLAUDE.md, "Release stage"). A Forum that applies the list states both members or neither; a client that meets `keyword_ignored` without `keyword_terms` prints no clause and never guesses one. The member joins R9.72 (this entry is unmerged, and the member is the same obligation as `keyword_ignored`: the Forum states what it did to the query); no new requirement number is taken. No conformance vector, no log row and no `curia-testis` path reads a search page; `db/` is untouched; `stopwords.txt` and its digest do not move.
