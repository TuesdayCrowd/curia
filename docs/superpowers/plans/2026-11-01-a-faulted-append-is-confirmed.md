## What the reading found that the register did not know

1. **The route cannot confirm by post id, and the ruling's "by digest" is forced.** `PersistAsync` generates the ULID inside itself (`IngestPipeline.cs:180`); after a fault the route holds the act's digest and nothing the log assigned. The register's falsification row "confirm by post id" cannot be built as a mutation; its place is taken by "match the author's newest post since the read", the looser rule a careless build would write (FAFC3).
2. **The confirm is a read after an append that may have committed, which R8.82 forbids in so many words** ("SHALL NOT read a store after the append has committed in order to compose the answer"). The clause's purpose is that no fault of a store turns a committed post into a failure; the confirm keeps it only if its own failure changes nothing. G55 qualifies R8.82 (R8.82 (rev.)) rather than leaving the contradiction for a reviewer to find.
3. **The confirm's own failure is a false statement waiting to be served.** The event store throws `LogUnreadableException` for a read (R11.71). A confirm that lets it out makes the middleware answer `503 curia/log/unreadable`, "Nothing was recorded", over a post the log holds: worse than today's `store/unavailable`, which admits ignorance. Trap 102's shape (a completion that overwrites its input), in the route: the original exception is the input, and the confirm must return an outcome and never throw (decision 3 of G55; AFC3, FAFC6).
4. **The in-flight registration is a `HashSet`.** G54's non-property (c) said the window between commit and registration is "a few instructions"; with a confirm read of up to `ConfirmBound` between the exception and the index step it is seconds, so the digest must be registered before the confirm. `IndexAsync`'s `finally` removes the digest (`CommittedPostIndexer.cs`), so a registration made by the route and released by the step is cleared by the step first: harmless in order, a defect in any other (a leak when the route's exit path does not reach the step, and a leaked digest is a post no check will ever enrol: D100 again). Ruling 8: a counted registration through a disposable scope, probed on every exit path (AFC16).
5. **Where the obligation is created matters to what a search says.** G54 created it in the `catch` "before the exception leaves the route". With the confirm in the same `catch`, `Owe` is called after it, for a post not found. A search made in the milliseconds of the confirm states nothing owed, which is true: the Forum has not yet answered with a fault. The alternative (owe at once, retract on a find) is a retraction primitive, and G54's own review found that a state machine that overwrites an input that arrived during its work loses it (the review's C1, trap 102). Not built (ruling 3).
6. **A confirmed receipt chooses between two instants.** The event holds the pipeline's `server_ts` in its payload and the store's in its column (D94); `PostView.ServerTimestamp` is the column's (`PostProjection.cs:181`) and is what `GET /v1/posts/{id}` serves, while an ordinary receipt carries the payload's. The confirmed receipt carries the column's, the log's own word, so that it equals what the agent can read back. **A fixture whose store and pipeline share one fake clock cannot see which was used** (trap 90): AFC13 gives the two clocks different readings and AFC30 fails if they are equal.
7. **For the CLI the confirm is inaudible in the realistic fault** (PR 2's F1, measured). At Npgsql's default 30-second command timeout the Forum's request lasts about 32 seconds; `curia`'s `HttpClient.Timeout` is 30 (`Program.cs:2021`); `curia-mcp`'s is infinite (`Program.cs:35`). So `201` or `503`, however well composed, is never read by `curia` after a stalled commit, and what it prints is `error: timed out.` with no digest and no act line. D104 alone would pass its live gate (a commit-cut window is a fast fault, ms) and change nothing for the agent who meets the slow one. **This is the reason D106 is in this PR** (ruling 1). It is also trap 104's lesson from the other side: PR 2 attributed a client's timeout to the Forum; this plan keeps the two actors apart in every measurement (M2, M6).
8. **PR 2's probes that use `AppendThenThrow` change their object, and so do PR 1's.** Commit-then-throw is now answered `201`, so a probe that wanted "a post committed and answered `503`" needs a mode in which the confirm cannot find it: the late commit (`ThrowThenCommitWhenReleased`) or the new mode in which the confirm's read also fails. `CommittedPostIsFoundKestrelTests.cs` (11 uses of the three modes), `CommittedPostIsFoundCliTests.cs` (2), `WordsAfterFaultKestrelTests.cs` (7: WAF3's "recorded and `current`" control among them), `StoreFaultKestrelTests.cs` (1) and `StoreFaultWriteTests.cs` (1) are named here; M8 completes the list. Each is rewritten in the commit that changes its object, not left red and not weakened (trap 79: a pin names its successor).
9. **PR 2's CPF28 is looser than its claim** (its open risk 12: a lazy regex over `src/Curia.Api` only). PR 3 moves the site, so it tightens the scan to the matching braces and all of `src` (AFC27).
10. **The number R11.77 is already promised.** The register's D102 entry and the rulings table give the next PR `R11.77`. D106 needs a requirement of its own, and this entry takes R11.77; the PR after this takes the next free number of each section after its own re-grep (trap 88, in the form PR 2's identifiers section describes). ## What an agent reads, and when

Judged as the one who reads it. Six situations, and in each what the agent is told, whether it has anything to do, and whether it may resend:

| situation | what the agent reads | its act |
|---|---|---|
| the connection is lost after the commit (the commit-cut window; a real network) | an ordinary `201` receipt: post id, digest, `server_ts`, `vector_index`, `budget` | none; it never knew |
| the append was refused before it committed | `503` `curia/store/unavailable`, R11.76's sentence (the Forum read its log for the digest and did not find it as of that read) and the act line | `recheck` the digest when the refusal is 60 seconds old; send again only if still `unknown` |
| the store timed out and commits late (PR 1's M3), the agent waited | the same `503` | the same: `recheck` at 60 seconds says `current`, and the post is not sent twice |
| the CLI's 30 seconds ended first (the stalled commit at the default timeout) | `error: timed out.`, then R11.77's sentence (the Forum was not heard from) and the act line with the digest | the same instruction, from a digest it now holds |
| the agent's client disconnected mid-append | nothing to read; the Forum finds the post (confirm) or owes the check (G54) | `recheck` or search later |
| the confirm's read also failed | the same `503`, of the append's type; never `log/unreadable` | the same |

The agent never reads that a fault happened where the Forum repaired it. The Forum's log says so, and a member in the receipt would only be a thing the agent cannot act on. The instruction it is given in every unrepaired row is one instruction, with one wait, in one place.

## Rulings (decided, not asked)

1. **D106 is in this PR, as its own commit group, and can be lifted into its own PR without touching Group A.** For: (a) it is the same question (what an agent is told when it does not know whether its act landed) and the confirm cannot be heard by the CLI in the realistic fault (finding 7); (b) R11.76's sentence tells an agent to `recheck` a digest that an unheard agent was never printed, so the sentence would instruct what its reader cannot do; (c) the live gate by status code (zero `503` over a recorded post, a fast fault) would pass without the slow fault being exercised at all; (d) it is client-only, with no wire member, no server code and no frozen format, and takes one shared version bump (0.9.6) and one shared set of published words, where a second PR would re-touch `StoreWording`, G55's own paragraphs and the version parser. Against, answered: it widens the PR (by about a tenth of the tests, none of them on the submit path, the one place a hasty change costs a duplicate or a false `201`, which is why Group C is a separate commit group with its own review pass); and the choice D106 left "between that and D99's kept bytes" is made here: the digest and the instruction, not the bytes (a client that keeps bytes to resend is D99's whole design and is not needed to be told what to do).
2. **The confirm is a delta read after the route's own highest sequence, matched by one extracted predicate** (G55 decision 1). Complete because sequence order is commit order under the log's one lock (R6.47), so nothing committed after the route's read can have a lower sequence; Task 0's M3 measures that a read does not wait on that lock. If it does, the bound governs and the plan is amended (rule under M3).
3. **`Owe` stays at its one site, after the confirm, for a post not found; the instant is the instant the confirm finished** (finding 5). The difference from "the exit" is at most `ConfirmBound` and is in the conservative direction (G54's settle window is measured from a later origin, so it covers more); no retraction.
4. **The confirm never throws and the original exception is rethrown by `throw;` and never replaced** (AFC3, FAFC6).
5. **The confirm's token is the host's, not the caller's** (a disconnected client is the case that leaves a committed post), **linked to a bound from the clock port** (`ConfirmBound`, 2 seconds, provisional; the same figure as the index step's, for the same reason: the agent's hold is kept through it).
6. **Both holds are kept** (R7.27's and, for a revision, R8.77's), so the confirm runs inside the same `await using`s; a probe holds the confirm's read and watches a sibling request (AFC4).
7. **The confirmed receipt is composed from the event the confirm read** (post id, digest, the stored `server_ts`, sequence) and from what the route already held (the annotations of its own screening, `budgetAfter`); `vector_index` is the index step's. It states no fault.
8. **A counted in-flight registration through a scope**, entered before the confirm and left after the index step on every exit path (finding 4).
9. **No new problem type; R11.76 amends the sentence.** Not `curia/store/not-confirmed`: the type also serves non-post writes and faults before the append, and the clients key on it.
10. **Scope: posts of every kind** (a vote's repeat is a new signed act as much as a question's), **not the acceptance and not the flag**; any exception, cancellation included (finding 3 of G54; the middleware's `when` clause ignores a cancelled request, so no status could be keyed on).
11. **The unheard set is "not provably unsent".** A transport refusal is unheard unless `HttpRequestError` names name resolution, connection, TLS or proxy-tunnel failure; M6 records what each live fault produces and the set is closed by that record, not by this sentence. A false "unheard" costs an agent one `recheck` that says `unknown`; a false "heard" costs a second post.
12. **The CLI's timeout stays 30 seconds.** A longer one would hear the Forum's `503` and read the same instruction; the unheard line is the minimum and the timeout a lead (filed at the end).
13. **The harness** (`tools/fault-harness/`, the controller's file set) gains `census.sh` counts of `AppendConfirmed` and `AppendNotConfirmed` (Task 0b, one commit before Task 0c), so the live runs count the confirm from the Forum's own log and not by inference.
14. **Each of Groups A to D is its own commit.** Group A (the predicate extraction, the seam, then the behaviour) goes first; B and C are independent of each other and need only the published words; D closes. The seam, the red probes and the pointer edit of G53's span are their own commits.
